Ross 308 vs Cobb 500: Which Broiler Chicken Breed Should You Grow?

Ross 308 and Cobb 500 are the two dominant broiler chicken breeds on Australian contracts. Here's how they actually compare — and why, in practice, most growers don't get to choose anyway.

Do growers choose the breed?

In practice, no — on almost every Australian broiler contract, the integrator (Ingham's, Baiada, BPL Adelaide) supplies day-old chicks of whichever breed they've standardised on, and growers don't select breed batch by batch. What matters for you as the grower is knowing which breed you've been supplied, so you're tracking your shed against the right growth curve rather than a generic target.

FAQ

Is Ross 308 or Cobb 500 better for FCR?

Both breeds are closely matched on FCR under modern genetics — differences on a given farm usually come down to management (feed, water, temperature) more than breed choice alone.

Which breed grows faster?

Ross 308 and Cobb 500 have very similar growth curves to day 42; Cobb 500 is often noted for slightly better breast meat yield, while Ross 308 is the most widely used breed across Australian integrator contracts.

Can I choose which breed to grow?

On most Australian contracts, the integrator supplies day-old chicks of their chosen breed — growers generally don't select breed themselves, but knowing the target curve still matters for tracking performance.
