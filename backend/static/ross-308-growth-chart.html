How to read this chart on an Aussie contract

1 · Body weight (kg)

Target average live weight of an as-hatched flock at the end of that day. If your day-7 sample weighs 195 g, you're 5 % behind the 205 g objective and should investigate feed intake, brooding temp or water access — quickly.

2 · Daily gain (g)

Expected weight gain that day. Ross 308 peaks around 95 g/day between day 27–34, then tapers. If daily gain in the finisher phase is under 85 g, temperature or ventilation is usually the culprit.

3 · Cumulative feed intake (kg)

Total feed a bird should have eaten by the end of that day. Multiply by number of birds placed to get expected silo draw — Broiler Base Mate does this automatically and alerts you when a shed drifts more than 5 % off target.

4 · Cumulative FCR

Feed Conversion Ratio = feed consumed ÷ live weight. Aviagen 2022 objectives are ambitious; typical Australian contract targets sit 0.03–0.08 higher because of climate and processing style differences. Use our FCR Calculator to convert your batch FCR to corrected FCR (cFCR).

FAQ — Ross 308 targets Aussie growers ask about

What is the target weight for Ross 308 at day 42?

Approximately 2.998 kg live weight as-hatched, with a cumulative FCR of about 1.52 per the Aviagen 2022 Performance Objectives. Australian contracts often catch a bit earlier (day 33–38) at 2.2–2.6 kg.

What weight should Ross 308 broilers be at day 21?

Around 1.012 kg live weight (as-hatched) with a cumulative FCR near 1.29.

What is the target FCR for Ross 308?

Cumulative FCR targets: day 21 ~1.29, day 28 ~1.36, day 35 ~1.44, day 42 ~1.52. Actual Australian contract targets sit 0.03–0.08 higher.

Is Ross 308 or Cobb 500 better for Australian contracts?

Ross 308 tends to deliver slightly better FCR at typical Aussie catch weights; Cobb 500 tends to deliver better breast yield at heavier weights. Your integrator usually picks — check your placement docket.
