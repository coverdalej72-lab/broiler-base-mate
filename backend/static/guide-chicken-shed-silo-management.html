Chicken Shed Silo Management 101

Getting silo feed ordering right on a chicken shed is a balance — order too late and you risk a run-out mid-batch, order too early and feed sits too long. Here's the practical rundown.

When to order feed

The safe rule of thumb: order when you have 3–5 days of feed remaining in the silo, based on current bird age and daily consumption. Consumption rises steadily as birds grow — a shed eating 400kg/day at day 10 might be eating 1,800kg/day by day 35 — so "days remaining" shrinks fast even without a change in silo level.

Avoiding feed spoilage

  • Don't overfill — feed sitting too long, especially in humid weather, can bridge (clump and stop flowing freely) or start to mould.
  • Rotate stock — where possible, use feed roughly in the order it arrived rather than letting older feed sit at the bottom.
  • Check for bridging after wet weather — a silo that suddenly stops flowing normally after rain is a common early warning sign.

Reading your feed-drop timing right

An empty feeder for even a few hours — especially during the grower phase when daily gain peaks — costs growth that's rarely fully recovered later in the batch. Matching feed drops to actual consumption curve (not a fixed weekly schedule) avoids both run-outs and over-fill waste.

Tracking multiple silos across sheds

On a multi-shed farm, each silo's days-of-feed can drift out of sync fast — one shed running ahead of schedule, another about to run dry. Checking every silo's remaining days at a glance (rather than walking the farm to eyeball levels) is the difference between a planned delivery and an emergency call to the feed mill.

FAQ

When should I order feed for a chicken shed silo?

Order when you have 3–5 days of feed remaining, based on current bird age and consumption rate — waiting until a silo is nearly empty risks a run-out if delivery is delayed.

How do I calculate days of feed remaining?

Days remaining = feed currently in the silo (kg) ÷ the shed's current daily consumption rate (kg/day), which rises steadily as birds age.

How does feed spoil in a silo?

Feed left too long, especially in humid conditions, can bridge (clump and stop flowing) or develop mould, both of which reduce effective feed available and can affect bird health.
