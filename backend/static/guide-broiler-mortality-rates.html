Broiler Chicken Mortality: Normal Rates & Red Flags

Every broiler chicken batch loses some birds — the question is whether it's a normal, expected loss or an early warning sign. Here's what a healthy mortality curve looks like on an Australian contract, and when to act.

Normal broiler mortality by benchmark

Batch outcomeTotal mortality %Notes
ExcellentUnder 3%Top-tier management, minimal disease/heat pressure

Where mortality happens in a batch

Mortality isn't evenly spread across the batch. The first 7 days (brooding) typically carry the largest single share of total mortality — cold stress, weak chicks, and early feed/water access problems all concentrate here. A second smaller bump often appears in the final week if heat stress or density issues build up as birds approach full size. A flat, steady trickle of mortality with no spikes is the healthiest pattern.

Red flags — when to act immediately

  • A sudden single-day jump — more than 0.5% of shed placement dying in one day outside the first week almost always means disease, a heat/cold event, or a feed/water outage.
  • Localised to one shed only — if one shed is running well above the others under identical conditions, check that shed's equipment (feeders, drinkers, ventilation) first.
  • Rising trend late in the batch — increasing daily mortality in the finisher phase often points to density or ventilation limits being reached.

Why mortality tracking matters for your payment

Mortality directly affects your total birds caught — and therefore your total settlement weight and FCR. Getting an accurate, up-to-date mortality count (not a guess at catch time) is essential for knowing your real batch numbers before settlement, not after.

FAQ

What is a normal mortality rate for a broiler batch?

A healthy 42-day Australian broiler batch typically finishes at 3–5% total mortality. Under 3% is excellent; over 6–7% warrants investigation.

When does most broiler mortality happen?

The first 7 days (brooding) typically account for the largest single share of total batch mortality, driven mostly by cold stress and early feed/water access issues.

What daily mortality count should trigger concern?

A sudden jump to more than 0.5% of shed placement dying in a single day — outside the normal first-week window — is a red flag worth investigating immediately.
